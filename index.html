Direction

My answer to a problem I see every day: there aren't enough people to do the work. Construction, agriculture and forestry are losing skilled operators faster than they can train new ones. Experienced operators are retiring, young people don't want dangerous, remote, physically hard jobs, and projects and harvests slip because nobody is available to run the machines. I've seen this directly, running my own business and working on machines myself.

Autonomy is how I want to close that gap. Not by replacing the few skilled people who are left, but by letting one operator do the work of several and handing the repetitive, dangerous tasks to the machine. Most fleets are old mixed-brand equipment that OEM autonomy will never reach, so I'm building toward a retrofit stack (perception, control and security by design) that makes existing machines semi-autonomous, starting with narrow repetitive tasks.

The three industries share the same problem and much of the same machinery: tractors, loaders, excavators and cranes doing repetitive work outdoors on rough terrain. Forestry is my testbed because I own the machines and the terrain. Construction (excavation, trenching, earthmoving, site logistics) and agriculture (field work, loading, material handling in tight seasonal windows) are the large markets the same stack moves into next.

Security is part of that product from day one. An autonomous 20-ton machine is an OT system on tracks: CAN bus, hydraulic controllers, PLCs and remote telemetry, most of it never designed to be networked. Once a machine can move without an operator, a compromised controller becomes a physical safety problem. My security background, focused on ICS/OT, lets me design the autonomy stack to be secure from the first line of code.
